  OUR  STORY  (Not  Yet  Decided)  Why  we  started  Buntii.  I  put  the  yellow  stickers  on.  Part-time,  I  work  the  markdown  floor  at  Sainsbury's,  the  trolley  of  food  that  has  to  go  by  tonight.  Perfectly  good  stuff,  cut  and  cut  again,  and  whatever  doesn't  sell  gets  thrown  out.  Every  shift.  It  does  your  head  in.  
I  lived  inBethnal  Green,  then  Edmonton  Green,  and  saw  the  other  side  of  it  on  every  high  street.  Turkish  greengrocers,  fishmongers,  bakers,  all  dropping  their  prices  as  the  evening  wore  on.  Trays  of  strawberries,  whole  sea  bass,  going  for  a  fraction  of  the  noon  price.  Except  nobody  knew  unless  they  happened  to  be  standing  right  there.  Meanwhile  everyone  I  know  is  watching  food  prices  climb,  nearly  forty  percent  in  five  years,  and  wondering  where  the  money  went.  And  seventeen  billion  pounds  of  good  food  goes  in  the  bin  every  year,  a  grand  of  it  from  the  average  family  of  four.  That's  a  mad  gap.  The  food  is  there.  The  prices  are  already  dropping.  People  need  it.  And  the  whole  thing  is  invisible.  Buntii  makes  it  visible.  Every  reduction  on  your  street,  the  independents  first,  the  shops  that  actually  know  you,  in  one  place,  in  real  time.  Not  a  mystery  bag.  Not  the  supermarket  only.  The  greengrocer,  the  fishmonger,  the  baker.  The  aunties  who  have  always  known  how  to  feed  a  family  for  less.  We're  starting  on  Green  Lanes,  the  densest  mile  of  independent  grocers  and  fishmongers  in  London.  But  North  London  is  the  wedge,  not  the  ceiling.  Every  high  street  in  this  country  has  the  same  shops  dropping  the  same  prices  to  nobody.  We're  going  to  switch  all  of  it  on.    Trust  your  auntie.
